\title[Good truncations for computing integral bases]{Good truncations for computing integral bases}
%\author{D. Basson, J. Boehm, S. Laplagne, M. Marais}

\begin{abstract}
We provide good truncation bound that speed up the computation of integral basis.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Introduction}
\label{section:introduction}

Integral basis are very useful in real life.

Given a polynomial $f \in K[x,y]$ monic in $Y$, we wish to compute the local contribution to the integral basis at the origin or the local integral basis at the origin.


\section{The determinacy}
\label{section:directApproach}

In this section we show:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An upper bound for the determinacy can be found fast reducing the polynomial modulo any prime number $p$.
\item If we truncate the polynomial $f$ by standard degree at the determinacy, the characteristic exponents of $f$ will not change.
\item The integrality exponent of $f$ can be computed from the characteristic exponents, so we can compute very fast the integrality exponent.
\end{enumerate}

Remark: the coefficients of the Puiseux expansions might change, but in all examples we tried, the coefficients don't change after truncation.

\section{Known results about precision of factorization and computation of Puiseux expansions}

We recall first two results that will be useful for our method.

The first result is about factorization over $K[[X]][Y]$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:factors}(See \cite[Proposition 23]{poteaux2021}.)
Let $F \in K[[X]][Y]$, separable of degree $d$. Suppose given a modular factorisation
\begin{equation}\label{factorization}
F \equiv  F_0 F_1 \dots F_s \mod X^{N_0}, \ N_0 > 2\kappa
\end{equation}
where $F_0 \in K[[X]][Y]$ is a unit and for all $i \ge 1$ either $F_i$ or its reciprocal polynomial $\tilde F_i$ is monic, and
$$
\kappa = \kappa(F_0,..., F_s) := \max_{I, J} \kappa(F_I, F_J),
$$
the maximum of the lifting orders being taken over all disjoint subsets $I, J \subset {0,\dots,s}$,
with $F_I = \prod_{i\in I} F_i$. Then there exists uniquely determined analytic factors $F_0^*, F_1^*,\dots, F_k^*$ such that $F = F_0^* F_1^* \cdots F_k^*$, where
$$
F_i^* \equiv F_i \mod X^{N_0-\kappa}.
$$
Moreover, starting from (\ref{factorization}), we can compute the $F_i^*$ up to any precision $N \ge N_0 - \kappa$ in $\bigO(dN)$ operations over $K$.
\end{proposition}

The authors provide the bound $\kappa \le \delta / 2$, where $\delta = \upsilon_X \left( \operatorname{Res}_Y(F, F_Y) \right) = 2 \IntExpX$, so $\kappa \le \IntExpX$.

The second result provides bound for the precision required for computing Puiseux expansions.
We use this algorithm taking $\mathbb{K}_P = \Q$.

We denote by \( (R_i)_{1 \le i \le \rho} \) the rational Puiseux expansions of \( H \). To any \( R_i \), we associate the following notations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $e_i$ is the ramification index (equal to the largest denominator or the number of expansions in the branch),
    \item $f_i$ is the residual degree,
    \item the \emph{regularity index} \( r_i \) of a Puiseux series \( R_i \) of \( F \) with ramification index \( e_i \) is the least integer \( N \ge \min(0, e \upsilon_X(R_i)) \) such that, if
\[
\left\lceil R_i \right\rceil^{N/e_i} = \left\lceil R' \right\rceil^{N/e_i}
\]
for some Puiseux series \( R' \) of \( F \), then \( R_i = R' \). We call \( \left\lceil R_i \right\rceil^{r_i/e_i} \) the \emph{singular part} of \( R_i \) in \( F \).
    \item we define \( v_i \in \mathbb{Q} \) as \( \upsilon_X(F_Y(S)) \), for any Puiseux series \( S \) associated to \( R_i \).

    If $H$ has only only one branch, then this number is the integrality exponent. Proof: if $F_i = (Y-\gamma_1) \dots (Y-\gamma_r)$ then $(F_i)_Y(\gamma_1) = (1)(\gamma_1 - \gamma_2)\cdots (\gamma_1 - \gamma_{e_i}) + 0 + 0 + \dots + 0$. The order of the product is equal to $\IntExpX$ (it is the same formula).

    If $H$ has many branches, then $v_i$ can be bigger than the integrality exponent.
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Half-RNP($H, P, n, \pi$)}
\label{algo:halfRNP}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} $P \in \mathbb{K}[Z]$ irreducible;
$H \in \mathbb{K}_P[X, Y]$ separable and monic in $Y$, with $d := \deg_Y(H) > 0$;
$n \in \mathbb{N}$ (truncation order); $\pi$ the current truncated parametrisation (initial call: $P = Z$, $\pi = (X, Y)$)
\State \textbf{Output:} All RPEs $R_i$ of $H$ such that $n - v_i \ge r_i$, with precision $(n - v_i)/e_i \ge r_i/e_i$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

There seems to be an error in the bounds here, which don't agree with the following theorem in that same paper:

\begin{lemma}(\cite[Lemma 3.19]{poteaux2021})
\label{lemma:pebound}
Let $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}$. To compute the RPE $R_i$ with certified precision
$n_0 > \frac{r_i}{e_i}$, it is necessary and sufficient to run \emph{Half-RNP}
with truncation bound $n = n_0 + v_i$. In particular, to ensure the computation
of the singular part of $R_i$, it is necessary and sufficient to use a truncation
bound $n > N_i$.
\end{lemma}

We use the bounds in the Lemma, which have been confirmed by the authors and by computational experiments.

To give general bounds for the precision $n$ required we define a new number:
$$
\alpha = \sum r_i,
$$
the sum taken over all Puiseux branches. This number is bigger than the integrality exponent but not much bigger.
For example, if we have only one branch with seven Puiseux expansions and one characteristic exponent $1/7$, then $\alpha = 1$ and the integrality exponent is $6/7$.

We have:
\begin{itemize}
\item $r_i \le \alpha$ (clear by definition)
\item $v_i \le \IntExpX \le \alpha$ when there is only one branch, but not in general.
\end{itemize}

Based on the last observation, we define $\tilde v_i = \upsilon_X((F_i)_Y(S))$, for any Puiseux series \( S \) associated to \( R_i \). This number will be used when we apply Algorithm \ref{algo:halfRNP} to only one branch of $F$.

\section{Local contribution to the integral basis at the origin}

In this section we focus on the computation of the local contribution at the origin (that is, we want to keep the unit too).

The steps are now as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Proposition \ref{prop:factors} says that if we truncate $F$ at precision $n$ in $X$ and we factorize the truncated polynomial $\bar F$ in $K[[X]][Y]$, we will get the factors of $F$ with precision $n - \kappa$.

\item
We are interested in computing the factors with precision at least equal to the integrality exponent in $X$ of $F$, we call this number $\IntExpX$. That is, we need
$n - \kappa \ge \IntExpX$.

\item In that paper the authors provide the bound $\kappa \le \delta / 2$, where $\delta = \upsilon_X \left( \operatorname{Res}_Y(F, F_Y) \right) = 2 \IntExpX$, so $\kappa \le \IntExpX$ and it is enough for us to consider $n \ge 2 \IntExpX$.

\item
For the computation of the integral basis we also need the singular part of the Puiseux expansions. If we want to compute the Puiseux expansions from the factors $F_1, \dots, F_s$, we would need to compute the factors with precision higher than the integrality exponent.

However if we compute the Puiseux expansions from $\bar F$, the same proposition \ref{prop:factors} says that we will obtain the Puiseux expansions with precision at least $\IntExpX$, and hence the singular part will be correctly computed.

\item I am not sure about this last step. In any case, we can compute the singular part of the Puiseux expansions by truncating at $3 \times \IntExpX$. The argument is as follows:

    \begin{enumerate}
    \item We apply Lemma \ref{lemma:pebound} to one factor $F_i$.
    \item In this case $v_i = v_X({(F_i)}_Y)$ and this is smaller than $\IntExpX$ (the integrality exponent of one branch is smaller than that of the full polynomial).
    \item By \cite[Lemma 3.21]{poteaux2021}, $r_i / e_i \le v_i$.
    \item By Lemma \ref{lemma:pebound}, we need $n \ge v_i + r_i/e_i$, and $v_i + r_i/e_i \le 2 \IntExpX$. That is, we need the factor $F_i$ developed to degree $2 \IntExpX$ and by Proposition \ref{prop:factors} we can get this precision by computing a factorization of $F$ modulo $3 \IntExpX$.
    \end{enumerate}

\item Putting everything together, we can truncate the powers of $X$ of $F$ at order $3 \IntExpX$ and we will be able to compute the correct factors up to the integrality exponent and the singular part of the Puiseux expansions and hence we will compute the correct integral basis.
\end{enumerate}

We obtain the following result.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:truncX}
Let $F \in K[X,Y]$ be monic in $Y$, let $\kappa$ be the maximum of the lifting orders as defined in Proposition \ref{prop:factors}.
Let $N_0 \in \N$, $N_0 > 3\IntExpX$, where $\IntExpX$ is the integrality exponent of $F$ in $X$ (the minimum exponent of a power of $X$ in the conductor of $F$).  Let $\overline F \in K[X,Y]$ be such that $F \equiv \overline{F} \mod X^{N_0}$. Then the generators of the local contribution at the origin to the integral basis of $\overline F$ are also generators of the local contribution at the origin to the integral basis of $F$. That is, for computing the local contribution at the origin to the integral basis of $F$ we can truncate the powers of $X$ of $F$ above degree $3 \IntExpX$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $F = F_0^* F_1^* \dots F_k^*$ be the analytic factorization of $F$ into branches.
To compute the local contribution at the original for the integral basis of $F$, we need to compute these factors developed up to the integrality exponent, and the singular part of the Puiseux expansions of $F$ at the origin. 

Let $\kappa$ be the maximum of the lifting orders as defined in Proposition \ref{prop:factors}. 
We know that $\kappa \le \delta / 2$, where $\delta = \upsilon_X \left( \operatorname{Res}_Y(F, F_Y) \right) = 2 \IntExpX$, so $\kappa \le \IntExpX$.

Since $N_0 > 3 \IntExpX$, in particular $N_0 > 2 \kappa$ and $N_0 - \kappa > 2\IntExpX$ and hence by Proposition \ref{prop:factors}, if we compute a factorization
$$
F \equiv  F_0 F_1 \dots F_s \mod X^{N_0},
$$
then $F_i^* \equiv F_i \mod X^{2 \IntExpX}$.

Finally, we follow Proposition \ref{lemma:pebound} to compute the singular part of the Puiseux expansions of $F_i$, $1 \le i \le k$, we need the factor $F_i$ computed with precision at least $\frac{r_i}{e_i} + v_i$. We have shown that $\frac{r_i}{e_i} + v_i \le 2\IntExpX$, so the result follows.
\end{proof}



\section{Local integral basis at the origin}

When we are interested in the local integral basis at the origin we can also truncate powers of $Y$.

One possible approach is the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Applying again \cite[Proposition 23]{poteaux2021} looking at the polynomial in  $K[[Y]][X]$ with high enough precision, we obtain that the factors at the origin are uniquely determined and they are the same factors as the factors obtained when we develop the factorization in terms of $X$ (but with a different parametrization), so we are able to recover the correct information.
\item The factor $g_0$ however corresponds to the branches of $f$ at $Y = 0$ and the factor $f_0$ corresponds to the branches of $f$ at $X = 0$, so we cannot recover the Puiseux expansions of $f_0$ from the Puiseux expansions of $g_0$.
\item Hence when we apply a truncation in $Y$ we can expect to compute correctly the local integral basis but not the local contribution.
\end{enumerate}

We now formalize these ideas and calculate the required precision. For this, we need to be able to go from Puiseux expansions in $X$ to Puiseux expansions in $Y$.  This can be done using inversion formulas, see for example \cite{Barroso}.

% ($N_0$ and $\kappa$ are the numbers to be determined, and they are possibly different for $X$-truncations and $Y$-truncations).

\begin{enumerate}
\item We start with a polynomial $F \in K[X,Y]$ with analytic factorization in $K[[X]][Y]$:
$$
F = F_0^* F_1^* \dots F_s^*,$$
where $F_k^* \in K[[X]][Y]$ and $F_0^*$ is the unit corresponding to the branches outside the origin). Let $d_k$ be the degree in $Y$ of $F_k^*$ and let $e_k$ be the ramification index of $F_k^*$ (the largest denominator of the Puiseux expansions).

\item If we have a factorization
\begin{equation} \label{FXfactors}
F \equiv  F_0 F_1 \dots F_s \mod X^{N_0},
\end{equation}
then by Proposition \ref{prop:factors}, $F_k \equiv F_k^* \mod X^{N_0 - \kappa}$.


\item Now we truncate in $Y$. Let $\overline{F}^{Y}$ be the truncation of $F$ modulo $Y^{M_0}$, for some value $M_0$ to determine. We can factorize this polynomial in $K[[X]][Y]$ as
$$
\overline{F}^{Y} \equiv  {H}_0 {H}_1 \dots {H}_s \mod X^{N_0},
$$
and we want to know if ${H}_k \equiv F^*_k \mod X^{N_0 - \kappa}$ for $k > 0$ (we cannot expect $H_0 \equiv F_0^* \mod X^{N_0 - \kappa}$).

\item We can also factorize $\overline{F}^{Y}$ in $K[[Y]][X]$:
$$\overline{F}^{Y} \equiv G_0 G_1 \dots G_s \mod Y^{M_0}.$$

\item Applying Proposition \ref{prop:factors} to ${F}$ in $K[[Y]][X]$  we obtain that
$$G_k \equiv G_k^* \mod Y^{M_0 - \kappa_Y}$$
where $G_k^*$ are the analytic factors of $F$ in $K[[Y]][X]$.

By the same argument $G_k$ agrees with the analytic factors of $\overline{F}^{Y} \mod Y^{M_0 - \kappa_Y}$.
Hence: the analytic factors in $K[[Y]][X]$ of $F$ and $\overline{F}^{Y}$ are the same modulo $Y^{M_0 - \kappa_Y}$.

\end{enumerate}

The question is what precision do we get when we go from these factors to factors in $K[[X]][Y]$.
We need to compute the factors correctly up to degree $\IntExpX$ in $X$, and we study now up to which degree we can truncate in Y.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $\overline{F}^{Y}$ be the truncation of $F$ at some degree $N_0$ in $Y$. We want to know a good $K$.
\item Assume $K \ge 3 \IntExpY$.
\item We can factorize $\overline{F}^{Y}$ in $K[[Y]][X]$:
$$\overline{F}^{Y} \equiv G_0 G_1 \dots G_s \mod Y^{N_0}.$$

\item By Proposition \ref{prop:factors}, since $K \ge 2 \IntExpY$, the factors are correct up to degree $n = N_0 - \IntExpY$. That is,
$$G_k \equiv G_k^* \mod Y^{N_0 - \IntExpY}$$
where $G_k^*$, $0 \le k \le s$, are the analytic factors of $F$ in $K[[Y]][X]$.

\item Consider the $k$-th branch. Let $\epsilon_k$ be the ramification index of $G^*_k$ (the largest denominator in the expansions), $\tilde v_k = v_Y((G^*_k(\eta(Y^{1/\epsilon_k})))$ and $r_k$ the regularity index (so that the singular part goes up to degree $r_k/\epsilon_k$). Hence
$$G^*_k = \prod_{i=1}^{\epsilon_k} (X - \eta(\xi^i Y^{1/\epsilon_k})).$$

We have
$$n - \tilde v_k \ge n - \IntExpY = N_0 - 2 \IntExpY \ge \IntExpY \ge \tilde v_k \ge \frac{r_k}{\epsilon_k}.$$

Then by Lemma \ref{lemma:pebound}, since the polynomial $G^*_k$ is computed with precision bound $n = N_0 - \IntExpY$, we obtain the Puiseux expansions with precision $n_0 = n - \tilde v_k \ge \IntExpY$. Let $D = n - \tilde v_k$.
\item The polynomial $\eta(U)$ corresponding to the $k$-th branch is correct up to degree $D\epsilon_k$: the Puiseux expansion $\eta(Y^{1/{\epsilon_k}})$ is correct to degree $D$ and we take $U = Y^{1/{\epsilon_k}}$.
\item Applying the inverse formula to the Puiseux expansions we get a polynomial $\gamma(T)$ which is correct up to the same degree $D\epsilon_k$ in $T$. Now we replace $T = X^{1/e_k}$ to get the Puiseux expansions in $X$, $\gamma(X^{1/e_k})$. We get correct Puiseux expansions up to degree $D\epsilon_k/e_k$.


\item As a conclusion, if we have a factorization
\begin{equation} \label{FXfactors}
F \equiv  F_0 F_1 \dots F_s \mod X^{N_0},
\end{equation}
then by Proposition \ref{prop:factors}, $F_k \equiv F_k^* \mod X^{N_0 - \kappa}$, where $F_k^* \in K[[X]][Y]$ are the analytic factors at the origin and $F_0^*$ is the unit corresponding to the branches outside the origin.

\item We need this number $D \epsilon_k / e_k$ to be bigger than $\IntExpX$. So we get $D \ge \frac{e_k}{\epsilon_k}\IntExpX$ and then
$$N_0 - \IntExpY - \tilde v_k \ge \frac{e_k}{\epsilon_k} \IntExpY.$$
So we get
$$N_0 \ge \frac{e_k}{\epsilon_k}\IntExpX + \IntExpY + \tilde v_k.$$
\item Consider $\frac{e}{\epsilon}$ the largest $\frac{e_k}{\epsilon_k}$ over all branches. So we need
$$N_0 > \frac{e}{\epsilon} \IntExpX + 2 \IntExpY,$$
which is the bound we were looking for.

\end{enumerate}

We obtain the following result

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:truncY}
Let $F \in K[X,Y]$ be monic in $Y$. Assume $F = F_0^* F_1^* \dots F_s^*$ is the analytic factorization of $F$, where $F_k^* \in K[[X]][Y]$ and $F_0^*$ is the unit corresponding to the branches outside the origin. Let $d_k$ be the degree in $Y$ of $F_k^*$ and let $e_k$ be the ramification index of $F_k^*$ (the largest denominator of the Puiseux expansions). Let $F = G_0^* G_1^* \dots G_s^*$ be the analytic factorization of $F$ in $K[[Y]][X]$ and let $\epsilon_k$ be the ramification index of $G_k^*$. Let $M_0 \in \N$, $M_0 > \frac{e_k}{\epsilon_k} \IntExpX + 2 \IntExpY$ for all $1 \le k \le s$ and $M_0 > 3 \IntExpY$. Let $\overline F \in K[X,Y]$ be such that $F \equiv \overline{F} \mod Y^{M_0}$. Then the generators of the local integral basis of $\overline F$ at the origin are also generators of the local integral basis of $F$ at the origin. That is, for computing the local the integral basis of $F$ at the origin we can truncate the powers of $X$ of $F$ at degree $M_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof} Let $F = G_0 G_1 \dots G_s$ be the factorization of $F$ modulo $Y^{M_0}$. 
By Proposition \ref{prop:factors}, we know that $G_i \equiv G_i^\star \mod Y^{M_0 - \IntExpY}$. 
Following \ref{lemma:pebound} (as in the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:truncX}), if we compute the Puiseux expansions of $G_i$, they will agree with the Puiseux expansions of $G^*_i$ up to degree at least $e/\epsilon \IntExpX$, where $e/\epsilon$ is the largest possible $e_k / \epsilon_k$, $1 \le k \le s$. 

If we apply the inversion formula to these expansions we will get the Puiseux expansions of $F_k$ in terms of $X$ correct up to degree $\epsilon_k/ e_k e/E \IntExpX \ge \IntExpX$. Hence we can also compute the correct factors $F_0^*, F_1^*, \dots, F_s^*$ up to degree $\IntExpX$, as wanted.
\end{proof}

We now formalize these ideas and calculate the required precision. For this, we need to be able to go from Puiseux expansions in $X$ to Puiseux expansions in $Y$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We start with a polynomial $F$ with analytic factorization
$$
F = F_0^* F_1^* \dots F_s^*$$
($F_i^* \in K[[X]][Y]$ and $F_0^*$ is the unit corresponding to the branches outside the origin).

\item Let $\overline{F}^X$ be the truncation of $F$ modulo $X^{N_0}$, for some $N_0 \in \N$. Applying any factorization algorithm to $\overline{F}^X$ (Newton-Puiseux algorithm or Hensel lifting) we can obtain a factorization
\begin{equation} \label{FXfactors}
F \equiv \overline{F}^X \equiv  F_0 F_1 \dots F_s \mod X^{N_0}.
\end{equation}


\item For this factorization, Proposition \ref{prop:factors} says that
$$F_i \equiv F_i^* \mod X^{N_0 - \kappa}$$
for $i > 0$.

\item Now we truncate in $Y$. Let $\overline{F}^{XY}$ be the truncation of $\overline{F}^X$ modulo $Y^{N_0}$. We can factorize this polynomial in $K[[X]][Y]$ as
$$
\overline{F}^{XY} =  \tilde{F}_0 \tilde{F}_1 \dots \tilde{F}_s,
$$
and we want to know if $\tilde{F}_i \equiv F_i \mod X^{N_0}$ for $i > 0$ (we cannot expect $\tilde{F}_0 \equiv F_0 \mod X^{N_0}$).

\item Applying again Proposition \ref{prop:factors} to $\overline{F}^X$ in $K[[Y]][X]$  we obtain that if we factorize $\overline{F}^{XY} \equiv  G_0 G_1 \dots G_s$, $G_i \in K[[Y]][X]$ and
$$
\overline{F}^{X} \equiv \overline{F}^{XY} \equiv  G_0 G_1 \dots G_s \mod Y^{N_0}
$$
then
$$G_i \equiv G_i^* \mod Y^{N_0 - \kappa}$$
for all $i$, where $G_i^*$ are the analytic factors of $\overline{F}^{X} \in K[[Y]][X]$.

\item Now the key argument is: for fixed $1 \le i \le s$, there is a reparametrization (or coordinates change) $(\alpha(X,Y), \beta(X,Y))$ that will transform $G_i^*$, into the corresponding analytic factor $(\overline{F}^{X})_i^*$ of $\overline{F}^{X} \in K[[X]][Y]$, that is
$$
G_i^*(\alpha(X,Y), \beta(X,Y)) = (\overline{F}^{X})_i^*(X,Y).
$$

Modulo $X^{N_0-\kappa}$, we obtain from (\ref{FXfactors}):
$$
G_i^*(\alpha(X,Y), \beta(X,Y)) \equiv (\overline{F}^{X})_i^* \equiv F_i \mod X^{N_0-\kappa}.
$$

We have to show that if we apply this same reparametrization to $G_i$ we will obtain
$$G_i(\alpha(X,Y), \beta(X,Y)) \equiv F_i \mod X^{N_0 - \kappa}.$$
\end{enumerate}
